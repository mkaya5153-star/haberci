Prenatal exposure to drug treatment for attention deficit/hyperactivity disorder and risk of neurodevelopmental disorders: Scandinavian multinational cohort study.Prenatal exposure to drug treatment for attention deficit/hyperactivity disorder and risk of neurodevelopmental disorders: Scandinavian multinational cohort study.

BMJ (Clinical research ed.) · Multicenter Study · Bruno C, Cesta CE, Hjellvik V, et al.

TÜRKÇE

OBJECTIVE: To assess whether prenatal exposure to drug treatment for attention deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) increases the risk of neurodevelopmental disorders in children. DESIGN: Population based cohort study. SETTING: Nationwide register data on births, dispensed drugs, and specialist healthcare diagnoses from Denmark, Norway, and Sweden. PARTICIPANTS: 3 643 376 children born in 2000-21 followed up for a median 9 years after birth in Sweden and 10 years in Norway and Denmark. INTERVENTIONS: Prenatal exposure, defined as ≥1 filled prescription for ADHD drug treatment anytime during pregnancy or in the second/third trimester (≥98 days after the last menstrual period).

Licensing mechanisms during RNA-guided DNA integration by type I-F3 CAST.Licensing mechanisms during RNA-guided DNA integration by type I-F3 CAST.

Nature · Fatma S, Dubey S, Truong VH, et al.

TÜRKÇE

Precise genomic integration of large DNA cargo remains a central challenge in genome engineering. CRISPR-associated transposases (CASTs)1,2 offer a promising solution by coupling RNA-guided DNA recognition to transposon-mediated integration. Because of their exceptional targeting fidelity, type I-F3 CASTs have been a major research focus1, yet the structural basis of their RNA-guided DNA integration has remained unclear. Here, using cryo-electron microscopy, computational protein design and functional assays, we report the licensing mechanisms that control DNA integration activity of Vibrio cholerae CAST (VchCAST).

Performance of intracortical microelectrode arrays in people with implanted brain-computer interfaces over a 20-year period.Performance of intracortical microelectrode arrays in people with implanted brain-computer interfaces over a 20-year period.

Nature medicine · Hahn NV, Stein E, Donoghue JP, et al.

TÜRKÇE

The silicon microelectrode 'Utah' array has enabled people with paralysis to control computer cursors, operate prosthetic limbs and communicate using high-performance brain-computer interfaces. However, reports so far have typically been limited to one or two individuals, with no systematic assessment of array longevity, decoding accuracy and day-to-day stability. Here we present a comprehensive 20-year evaluation of neural data from the BrainGate clinical trials, spanning 2,289 recording sessions, 20 arrays and 14 participants.
