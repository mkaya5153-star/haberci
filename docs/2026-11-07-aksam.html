Intravenous Thrombolysis for Acute Ischemic Stroke - New Directions.Intravenous Thrombolysis for Acute Ischemic Stroke - New Directions.

The New England journal of medicine · Review · Grotta JC

TÜRKÇE

In the past 5 years, substantial changes have been made in the use of intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) in patients with acute ischemic stroke. Integrating IVT with endovascular thrombectomy, which improves outcomes in patients with stroke due to large cerebral vessel occlusion, has been a particular challenge. This review discusses the underlying vascular targets for IVT and thrombectomy, the increasing use of tenecteplase instead of alteplase, broadening indications for the use of IVT, and ancillary drugs and dosing methods that may augment the success of IVT.

Eating disorders.Eating disorders.

Lancet (London, England) · Review · Schmidt U, Austin A, Ehrlich S, et al.

TÜRKÇE

The eating and feeding disorders are serious psychiatric disorders at the mind-body interface, often with an exceptionally high burden for affected individuals, their families and supporters, and wider society. In anorexia nervosa, high acute medical risk and patients' ambivalence about treatment can make a challenging combination that can impede engagement. First-line treatments for eating disorders are mainly psychological, with medications playing an adjunctive role. Novel biological and technology-based interventions are only slowly emerging. Compared with other psychiatric disorders, research into these disorders is seriously underfunded.

The first 1000 days: Modeling language learning from individual children's everyday speech.The first 1000 days: Modeling language learning from individual children's everyday speech.

Science (New York, N.Y.) · Raviv H, Tsyhanov A, Gousios K, et al.

TÜRKÇE

Children learn to understand and speak from the speech around them, yet how that everyday input shapes learning remains unresolved. Large language models show that language is learnable from data, but their scale and training conditions differ sharply from a child's natural experience. We trained cognitively feasible, child-specific models on longitudinal home recordings from eight children across their first 1000 days of life. Each model learned incrementally from a single child's ordered speech input without prior linguistic knowledge, developing phoneme-like and word-form representations that tracked each child's learning pace.
