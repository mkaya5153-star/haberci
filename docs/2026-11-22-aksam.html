Spontaneous coronary artery dissection: implications for women's health, clinical care, and future research.Spontaneous coronary artery dissection: implications for women's health, clinical care, and future research.

Lancet (London, England) · Review · Bay B, Magnussen C, Adlam D, et al.

TÜRKÇE

Spontaneous coronary artery dissection (SCAD) is an increasingly recognised cause of acute coronary syndromes, characterised by spontaneous separation of the layers of the coronary arterial wall. It predominantly affects younger women, and recent registry and imaging studies indicate that SCAD is more common than previously appreciated, challenging its historical classification as a rare condition. Clinically, SCAD is associated with a substantial early risk of adverse events, particularly in the first weeks after the index dissection.

A neural code for generative speech production in human Wernicke's area.A neural code for generative speech production in human Wernicke's area.

Science (New York, N.Y.) · Silva AB, Leonard MK, Hullett PW, et al.

TÜRKÇE

Wernicke's area in the posterior temporal lobe is classically defined by its role in speech comprehension, yet a paradox remains: Why does damage here also profoundly impair speech production? Using direct human brain recordings, cortical stimulation, and lesion analysis, we found that this region is not solely for perception. A discrete subzone within Wernicke's area, the left posterior superior temporal gyrus (lpSTG), becomes active before speech, is causally necessary for speech production, and codes features of whole upcoming words rather than low-level acoustics or articulatory information.

A thalamo-amygdalar circuit drives female vulnerability to chronic pain after early-life stress.A thalamo-amygdalar circuit drives female vulnerability to chronic pain after early-life stress.

Science (New York, N.Y.) · Borruto AM, Kohlund T, Rettig L, et al.

TÜRKÇE

Childhood adversity increases the risk of chronic pain and anxiety later in life, particularly in women, yet the neural mechanisms linking early-life stress to adult vulnerability remain elusive. Here, we show that social isolation and nerve injury interact to amplify activity-dependent synaptic gain and anticipatory recruitment of the paraventricular thalamus-to-central amygdala pathway in female, but not male, mice. This female-specific circuit sensitization enhances mechanical hypersensitivity and avoidance behaviors in adulthood.
